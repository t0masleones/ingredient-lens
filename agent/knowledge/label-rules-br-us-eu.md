# Label rules cheat sheet (BR / US / EU) - v0.2

Simplified summary used to cross-check what a label declares. Not legal advice.

Status (2026-10-16): Brazil, United States and European Union items were checked against the sources listed at the bottom. Diet claims in Brazil and the EU are still TO VERIFY.

If a label seems to break a rule, say "worth double-checking". Never accuse a manufacturer.

## Brazil (labels usually in Portuguese)

The labeling rules below were consolidated into RDC 727/2022 (Anvisa) without changes to their substance. The original numbers (RDC 26/2015, RDC 135 and 136/2017) are still widely cited. Anvisa opened a targeted revision of the allergen rules in 2025 to converge with Codex CXS 1-1985: check for a newer rule before citing a number.

### Allergens - RDC 26/2015, consolidated in RDC 727/2022
- Covers foods and drinks, including ingredients and additives, packaged without the consumer present.
- Required statements, grouped in one sentence right after or below the ingredient list: "Alérgicos: Contém ...", "Alérgicos: Contém derivados de ..." or "Alérgicos: Contém ... e derivados".
- Possible cross-contact: "Alérgicos: Pode conter ...", used when the absence of cross-contact cannot be guaranteed.
- Format: upper case, bold, contrasting color, minimum height of 2 mm and never smaller than the ingredient list letters.
- Crustaceans: the statement must include the common name of the species.
- Milk of all mammal species counts as an allergen (goat milk, for example).
- Claims about the absence of allergenic foods are not allowed, except in cases the rule provides for.

### Gluten - Lei 10.674/2003
- All industrialized foods must show "CONTÉM GLÚTEN" or "NÃO CONTÉM GLÚTEN", in prominent, clear and legible characters.
- State health inspectors consider a "não contém glúten" claim inconsistent when the label also says the product contains or may contain wheat, barley, rye or oats, or may contain traces of gluten. Report this as a mismatch.

### Lactose - RDC 135/2017 and RDC 136/2017, consolidated in RDC 727/2022
- "Zero lactose", "isento de lactose", "0% lactose", "sem lactose" or "não contém lactose": at most 100 mg of lactose per 100 g or mL of the ready-to-eat food.
- "Baixo teor de lactose": more than 100 mg and up to 1 g per 100 g or mL.
- Foods with more than 100 mg of lactose per 100 g or mL must declare "Contém lactose" right after or below the ingredient list. RDC 136 has additional thresholds for some product categories; see the source.
- Lactose-free does not mean milk-protein-free.

### Additives - IN 211/2023 (Anvisa)
- Lists the authorized additives with their INS number, function, limits and conditions of use. It is amended often (Agenda Regulatória theme 3.34).
- On labels, an additive appears as its function plus its name or INS number, for example "emulsificante lecitina de soja" or "estabilizante INS 322".

## United States
- Major food allergens: milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. FALCPA (2004) defined the first eight; the FASTER Act made sesame the ninth from January 1, 2023.
- Declaration: the food source must appear at least once, either in parentheses after the ingredient ("whey (milk)") or in a "Contains" statement right after or next to the ingredient list ("Contains wheat, milk, and soy"). The specific tree nut and the species of fish and crustacean must be named.
- FDA allergen guidance, Edition 5 (January 2025): 12 tree nuts remain major allergens (almond, cashew, pecan, pistachio, walnut and others). Coconut, shea, kola and some others were removed, so "Contains: tree nuts" may leave them out, but they still appear in the ingredient list. Milk now includes sheep and goat milk; egg includes duck, goose and quail eggs.
- "May contain" statements are voluntary and not required by law. When used they must be truthful and not misleading.
- "Gluten-free" (21 CFR 101.91): no gluten-containing grain (wheat, rye, barley and their crossbreeds) unless processed to remove gluten, and less than 20 ppm gluten. "No gluten" and "without gluten" follow the same rule. Oats are not a gluten-containing grain under this rule.

## European Union
- Regulation (EU) 1169/2011, Annex II: 14 allergens. Cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt, kamut), crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk (including lactose), tree nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews and others), celery, mustard, sesame, sulphur dioxide and sulphites above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/L as SO2, lupin, molluscs.
- Article 21: the allergen must be emphasized in the ingredient list by a typeset that clearly distinguishes it (font, style or background color). A separate "Contains" statement is not the main mechanism.
- Regulation (EU) 828/2014: "gluten-free" means at most 20 mg/kg of gluten as sold; "very low gluten" means at most 100 mg/kg, only for foods with specially processed wheat, rye, barley or oats.
- E numbers come from the Union list in Annex II of Regulation (EC) 1333/2008. E and INS numbers are usually the same number.
- The rules engine covers the allergens shared with Brazil and the US. Celery, mustard, sulphites and lupin are not checked yet: mention them yourself for EU labels.

## Diet claims
- United States: the FDA does not define or regulate "vegan". The claim only has to be truthful and not misleading, and third-party vegan certification is voluntary. Treat a vegan claim as supporting evidence, not proof.
- Brazil and EU: not covered in this cheat sheet yet (TO VERIFY).
