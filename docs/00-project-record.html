1. Summary

Ingredient Lens is an AI agent that reads an ingredient list (label photo, typed text or recipe) and tells the user, in plain language, whether it fits their restrictions: intolerances, allergies, celiac disease and vegan or vegetarian diets. It also cross-checks the ingredient list against the label's own statements and sends contradictions to human review.

Version 0.1 was built as a no-code Claude Project (instructions plus knowledge files). The first hands-on test raised a scope question: reading a label is something any general-purpose LLM can do, so the prototype on its own did not show much business value. Version 0.2 answers that by moving the decisions that must be repeatable into tools: a rules engine, a governed ingredient database, a human-review queue and an audit log, connected to the agent through an MCP server.

Version 0.3 widens the data. 10,902 terms from the public Open Food Facts taxonomies (ODbL) sit behind the 430 curated terms, with a conservative rule: they give diet status only, and an allergen is ruled out only with evidence. The US and EU label rules were checked against official sources.

2. Project at a glance

ProblemPeople who must avoid ingredients have to decode small, technical labels. Mistakes cause discomfort, health risk or a broken promise to a guest. Regulatory and quality analysts do repetitive first-pass checks of labels against their own statements.
ObjectiveA trustworthy first-pass screening agent that flags what matters, says what it does not know, and hands contradictions to a human.
PersonasShopper with intolerances · Person with low literacy or low vision · Cook serving guests with restrictions · Regulatory or quality analyst. See 01 · Discovery.
In scopeLabel photos, text and recipes; allergen and intolerance flags; animal vs plant origin; vegan and vegetarian checks; label statement cross-check; human-review handoff.
Out of scopeMedical advice; legal or regulatory rulings; live company data; user accounts; barcode lookup; nutrition; halal and kosher.
ConstraintsZero cost. Public or invented examples only; no company or customer data. Screening aid, not advice.

6. To-be process (v0.3)

As-is processes are in 01 · Discovery.

flowchart LR
  U[User: label photo or text] --> A[Agent: transcribe exactly, mark unsure words]
  A -->|unreadable| R[Ask for a better photo]
  A --> T[check_label tool]
  T --> DB[(Ingredient DB: curated + Open Food Facts)]
  T --> X[Rules: profile, STRICT, XC1-XC4]
  X --> E[Agent: explain in plain words]
  X -->|escalate| Q{User agrees?}
  Q -->|yes| K[create_review_ticket]
  K --> H[Human reviewer]
  T --> L[(Audit log)] --> M[usage_stats]

Assumptions

  • Users provide the label (photo or text); there is no product database or barcode lookup.
  • Brazilian rules (RDC 26/2015, Lei 10.674/2003, RDC 135 and 136/2017, consolidated in RDC 727/2022) are the primary market.
  • A first-pass screening that escalates contradictions saves expert time even if it never gives the final answer.

Issues

  • Claude Desktop is connected, but no chat test with the tools has been recorded yet.
  • The consolidated RDC 727/2022 PDF could not be read automatically (HTTP 403), so the list of rules it revokes was not confirmed.
  • Pain points in 01 · Discovery are still TODO; no user interview or UAT done.
